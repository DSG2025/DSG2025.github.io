Your first day in Saigon: what to expect

A practical first-day guide to Ho Chi Minh City: traffic, rain, heat, airport arrival, transport, walking and how to avoid overplanning your first 24 hours.

Your first day does not need to be your busiest day. Saigon is easier to enjoy when you leave some space for the heat, traffic, a long airport arrival, an unexpected rain shower or a coffee that lasts longer than planned.

First: the city feels fast before it feels familiar

Traffic is usually the first thing new visitors notice. Motorbikes move through gaps that may not look like gaps, pavements can change from one block to the next, and crossing a road can feel more complicated than the map suggested.

Give yourself a little time to watch how people move. Use marked crossings where available, move predictably and avoid sudden changes of direction. If a road feels uncomfortable, there is no prize for forcing the crossing immediately.

Do not build a 10-stop first-day itinerary

Distances on a map can be misleading. A short distance can take longer because of traffic, heat, rain, parking or simply the amount of street life that slows you down in a good way.

For the first day, choose one or two priorities and keep the rest optional. If you arrive on a long-haul flight, even less may be better.

Rain is normal travel weather, not automatically a ruined day

Tropical rain can arrive hard and fast. Sometimes it passes quickly; sometimes it stays around. A flexible plan works better than spending the whole day checking the sky.

For walking and food experiences, a shower may simply mean stopping under an awning, changing the order of a route or waiting with a drink. Good footwear matters more than trying to stay perfectly dry.

Heat changes how much you can comfortably do

Drink water, wear light clothing and build indoor or shaded breaks into the day. Travellers often underestimate how tiring a combination of heat, jet lag and walking can be.

If you plan a historical site such as Cu Chi, remember that much of the visit is outdoors. If you plan a city day, alternating between streets, cafés, museums and transport can make the pace easier.

Airport arrival: keep your first hour simple

After landing at Tan Son Nhat Airport, immigration, bags and traffic can make exact timing difficult. If you arrange a pickup, send the flight number and keep a working contact method available after landing. If you arrange your own transport, use an official service or a reputable ride-hailing option and confirm the destination before leaving.

Do not schedule something important immediately after an international arrival unless you have enough buffer for delays.

Cash, cards and your phone

Cards are common in many hotels, restaurants and larger businesses, while small food stalls and local purchases may be easier with Vietnamese cash. Keep smaller notes available rather than relying on a large denomination for every purchase.

A working mobile data connection is especially useful for maps, translation, ride-hailing and messaging your hotel or host.

What makes a good first evening?

A neighbourhood walk, simple local food and a conversation can be a better introduction than racing through landmarks. The point is to start reading the rhythm of the city: where people sit, what stays open, how alleys connect to main roads and how quickly the atmosphere changes after dark.
